## Strategy and result

I began by collecting central positions and building threats across several small boards. The important decisions were usually about routing: a locally attractive move could send the opponent straight to a capture. I often left a local threat unblocked when blocking it would give away something more important elsewhere.

My early buildup was slow to convert. No small board was captured until move 173, and the bot eventually converted its pressure into the first won outer region. Later, I used routes into completed regions to obtain unrestricted moves and convert prepared threats into outer-region wins.

## Assessment of the bot

The bot showed useful tactical awareness. It took captures, made local blocks, and often avoided blocks whose destinations would reward X. Its strongest observed sequence was the routing trap that forced ownership of outer region 9.

The clearest weakness was its final move. It protected a small board while leaving an immediate whole-game loss unblocked. That is a concrete tactical failure in this position. This match alone does not establish whether search timing, move ordering, evaluation, or another implementation detail caused it.

My play was not demonstrated optimal either: the early central buildup left threats unconverted for a long time and allowed O to win the first outer region. One match demonstrates specific strengths and a specific failure, not an overall win rate.
